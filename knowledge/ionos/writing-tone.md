## voice_register

- (95) Maintain a high, corporate formality that acts as a reliable partner rather than a casual friend.
- (90) Write with confident and engaging authority, being bold about the value provided without ever sounding pushy or arrogant.
- (90) Maintain a friendly, helpful tone that focuses on the audience's goals and needs without ever sounding condescending.
- (90) Use active phrasing to directly involve the reader and prompt action, rather than passive constructions.
- (90) Address the reader directly using active phrasing and a formal, premium stance.
- (90) Adopt an authoritative, visionary, and reassuring tone that speaks with the weight of an established industry leader.
- (90) Use professional, industry-standard terminology instead of slang, colloquialisms, or informal greetings.
- (80) Use an encouraging and inspiring tone that shows ambition for the customer's success.
- (80) Use active, confident verbs that imply progress and stability.

## web_readability

- (90) Keep sentences simple, use easily understandable words, and avoid excessive decorative verbs to support skimming.
- (90) Structure texts with clear headings and front-load the most important information at the very beginning of the headline.
- (90) Avoid distracting embellishments, nested sentences, and sales-driven exaggerations to keep the writing as simple as possible.
- (90) Avoid using questions in headlines; provide answers and solutions directly instead.
- (90) Keep sentences concise, minimalist, and structurally clean to prevent obscuring the main message.
- (80) Place SEO keywords at the very beginning or end of headlines, provided the sentence structure remains natural.
- (80) Always explain and contextualize technical jargon if its use is unavoidable.

## ux_microcopy

- (100) Write error messages that state what happened and how to fix it, strictly avoiding humor, panic-inducing language, or blaming the user.
- (90) Provide UX copy on a strict need-to-know basis, giving users only enough information to complete the task at hand.
- (90) Keep tooltips user-focused and task-oriented, minimizing marketing language to strictly provide background information or context.
- (90) Keep image alt-text brief and omit it entirely if the image does not provide additional context to the page.
- (85) Use highly functional and instructional language for system messages, such as 'Document moved to Brand Portal'.

## cta_conventions

- (90) Write calls to action that actively tell the user what will happen next, ideally using a strong verb.
- (90) Keep CTA buttons to a maximum of three words, use dynamic verbs to convey urgency, and never use simple yes or no choices.
- (70) When a call to action is integrated as a text link within body copy, the three-word limit does not apply.

## audience_framing

- (100) Frame messages positively and optimistically, strictly avoiding marketing tactics based on fear or user insecurity.
- (95) Focus on tangible professional benefits and technical excellence rather than soft, vague emotional appeals.
- (90) Keep the focus on the customer by minimizing first-person company references and emphasizing how the user is enabled.
- (90) Focus on positive messages and simple solutions rather than highlighting the user's lack of IT skills or dwelling on problems.
- (85) Frame communication as a precise, high-quality tool focused on the user's specific goals and technical reliability.

## misc

- (80) Prioritize high-quality, substantial content over keyword stuffing or short-term SEO traffic gains.
